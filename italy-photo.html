Roll Labeling Protocol
How to Label a Shot Roll
Format:   R## · City/City · D#-# · MMDD
R03 · Venice/Florence · D4-5 · 0609
R## — Sequential roll number for the trip
City/City — Where the roll was shot. One city if it stayed, two if it crossed. In order.
D#-# — Trip days covered by this roll
MMDD — Date the roll finished (when you apply the label)
Label is applied after the roll is shot — not at load. Records what happened, not what was planned. Tool: white gaffer tape + Sharpie.
Photography · City by City
Italy in June — Hard Mediterranean sun 10am–4pm. Golden hour arrives late (~8:30–9pm) and is long and warm. Early morning in narrow streets is soft and directional. Ektacolor handles highlights gently — expose for shadows, let highlights roll off naturally.

Film Mindset · Italy
You have 15 rolls. That's 540 frames. Shoot freely — you will not run out.
Metering hierarchy: MP internal meter is the constant. Lightme via quick action button for a fast second opinion. 308X when you're stationary and have real time...a restaurant interior, a composed shot, a deliberate setup.
iPhone reference shot is per location, not per frame. One shot that anchors where you were and what the light looked like. Camera roll + GPS + timestamp is the passive record. That's enough.
Ektacolor handles highlights gently. In hard Italian midday sun — expose for the shadows, let the highlights roll off. The film will hold it.
The 28mm is your eye. It's always on. Only reach for 35mm or 50mm when you feel it — don't think about it.
You're not slowing the family down. You're documenting them. The camera is already around your neck. The shot takes 2 seconds. Trust that.
