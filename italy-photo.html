Roll Labeling Protocol
How to Label a Shot Roll
Format:   R## · City/City · D#-# · MMDD
R03 · Venice/Florence · D4-5 · 0609
R## — Sequential roll number for the trip
City/City — Where the roll was shot. One city if it stayed, two if it crossed. In order.
D#-# — Trip days covered by this roll
MMDD — Date the roll finished (when you apply the label)
Label is applied after the roll is shot — not at load. Records what happened, not what was planned. Tool: white gaffer tape + Sharpie.
Photography · City by City
Italy in June — Hard Mediterranean sun 10am–4pm. Golden hour arrives late (~8:30–9pm) and is long and warm. Early morning in narrow streets is soft and directional. Ektacolor handles highlights gently — expose for shadows, let highlights roll off naturally.
⚠ Itinerary sync — Photography tips below are matched to country-index.html as of May 4, 2026. If the Italy trip page itinerary changes, update this page to match. Check city activities, day numbers, and Leica store details.
Milan
Days 1–2
Jun 6–7 · 2 nights
Day 1 evening — Piazza del Duomo + Galleria passeggiata
Arrival day. Arriving 2:40pm — by 6pm you're settled and out. Evening light on the Duomo facade is warm and directional. The Galleria interior has soft diffused dome light at any hour. Family walking through — 28mm, let them move, don't pose. This is your first roll.
🌅 Evening · 6–9pm · first frames of the trip
Day 2 morning — Duomo rooftop
Pre-booked. ~€15. The rooftop gives you the city spread at eye level with the spires. Family framed against Milan skyline. 28mm — get close, include the Gothic detail. Go early before the heat and crowds build.
🌅 Morning · best before 10am
Day 2 — Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II
Adjacent to the Duomo — same loop, no detour. Iron-and-glass dome. Katrin's first boutique stop. The geometry of the arcade floors and the light from above is a repeatable frame. 28mm looking up, or tight on someone looking at a window.
☁ Soft diffused · works any time
Day 2 afternoon — Train to Venice after aperitivo lunch
Aperitivo before catching the train. Outdoor table, afternoon light, the family at ease. This is a quiet documentary moment — don't overthink it. Shoot through lunch.
🌤 Relaxed pace · afternoon train

Venice
Days 3–4
Jun 8–9 · 2 nights
Day 3 — Vaporetto arrival on Grand Canal
Take Line 1 — slower, more scenic, goes the full length of the Grand Canal. Camera out from the moment you board. The palazzi, the water light, the family seeing Venice for the first time. X100VI earns its place here — one hand, bags in the other, moving boat.
🌊 Water light · arrival afternoon
Day 3 — Wander the sestieri + Rialto area
No agenda day — best photography day in Venice. Getting lost is the plan. Cannaregio, Dorsoduro, the bridges. Rialto market area has a local energy even in the afternoon. Family pausing at canals, looking at maps, ducking under washing lines. This is your documentary mode.
🌅 Late afternoon into evening · best light
Day 4 early — St. Mark's Basilica before 9am
Pre-booked entry. The mosaics inside are extraordinary in low interior light — M11P handles this better than the MP. Exterior of the Basilica and the Piazza at this hour still has some softness. By 10am it's wall-to-wall tourists.
🌅 Before 9am · essential
Day 4 afternoon — Murano glass demo + gondola at dusk
Murano: Vaporetto 4.1 or 4.2. The glass blowing demo — Jayden and Kai engaged and lit by furnace glow. A rare indoor light situation that's genuinely interesting. Then back for the gondola at dusk — negotiate directly with gondoliers. ~€90/6 people. 28mm, shoot from inside the gondola looking out and up at the buildings.
🌅 Gondola at dusk · 7–8pm · film moment

Florence
Days 5–6
Jun 10–11 · 2 nights
Day 5 afternoon — Oltrarno + Ponte Vecchio
South side of the Arno. Artisan workshops, trattorias, narrow streets. Slower pace. Ponte Vecchio: don't shoot from it — shoot it from Ponte Santa Trinita to the west. Family walking, the bridge behind them, the Arno catching afternoon light. 35mm works here — slightly more compression than 28mm.
🌅 Late afternoon · warm Arno light
Day 5 — Leica Store Florence · Vicolo dell'Oro
Joel's audible. Small street literally steps off Ponte Vecchio — folds naturally into the Oltrarno afternoon with zero detour. Check hours before going. This is on the itinerary — make it happen.
📍 Leica · Vicolo dell'Oro · steps from Ponte Vecchio
Day 6 early — Pisa day trip · Leaning Tower
Regional train from SMN, ~1hr. The Tower climb is the experience (pre-booked, ~€18/person). But the family frame at the Piazza dei Miracoli — everyone doing the holding-up-the-tower pose — that's the documentary shot. Shoot it earnestly. It's a family memory, not a cliché.
🌅 Morning · back to Florence by early afternoon
Day 6 — Brunelleschi's Dome climb + Uffizi
463 steps — Jayden and Kai will race each other. Frame them from behind climbing, or from above looking down into the stairwell spiral. Then Uffizi late afternoon — the galleries are dim and controlled. M11P for interiors. Look for Katrin and the boys in front of the Botticelli — the painting dwarfs them. That's the shot.
🌤 Afternoon museum light

Rome
Days 7–9
Jun 12–14 · 3 nights
Day 7 afternoon/evening — Trastevere
Arrival day. Cobblestone streets, ivy-covered walls, warm restaurant light. This is your documentary neighborhood for the whole trip — return here every evening if you can. Day arrival: soft afternoon light in the narrow streets. Evening: M11P takes over. The spill of warm trattoria light onto cobblestones at 9pm is what this camera was made for.
🌙 Evening primary · M11P · 28mm
Day 8 morning — Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel
Earliest entry slot — June crowds are intense by 10am. Sistine Chapel: no flash, technically no photography, everyone does it. The real shot is the family overwhelmed by the scale — look up at them looking up. M11P for low interior light. St. Peter's is free entry right after — the colonnade and piazza at midday has dramatic shadow geometry.
☀ Interior low light → M11P · exterior midday geometry
Day 8 afternoon — Leica Store Rome · Via dei due Macelli 57
Joel's confirmed stop. Short Uber from Prati (~10 min). Via dei due Macelli is a 3-min walk from Trevi Fountain — folds naturally into the afternoon loop. Typically 10am–7pm. Then Trevi → Spanish Steps → gelato. The whole pocket is walkable in 2hrs.
📍 Leica · Via dei due Macelli 57 · 3 min from Trevi
Day 8 — Trevi Fountain + Spanish Steps loop
Trevi: the family throwing coins. Shoot from the side — not straight on like every tourist photo. The chaos and the water and their faces. Spanish Steps late afternoon are less crowded. 28mm looking up the steps with the church behind. Gelato somewhere in between.
🌅 Late afternoon · less crowded · better light
Day 9 morning — Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill
One combo ticket, pre-booked essential. Full morning — 3-4hrs. Hard June light is actually interesting here — the ruins cast dramatic shadows. Meter for the shadow side of the family's faces, let the ruins blow slightly. Then Testaccio for lunch — Rome's most authentically local food neighborhood.
☀ Hard light works for ruins · shadow geometry
Day 9 afternoon — Free time · Piazza Navona
No agenda afternoon. Piazza Navona has fountains, street artists, outdoor cafes. Low-pressure wandering — the best conditions for reactive shooting. Let the family set the pace. Follow, don't lead.
🌤 Relaxed · reactive · follow the family

Salerno · Amalfi
Days 10–11
Jun 15–16 · 2 nights
Day 10 afternoon — Lungomare Trieste waterfront
Salerno's main promenade along the Gulf of Salerno. This is a real Italian city — not a tourist destination. Locals, kids on scooters, elderly couples walking. The Gulf and the hills behind everything. This is the Lisbon waterfront equivalent — your kind of shot. Decompress after Rome. Shoot slowly.
🌅 Late afternoon · locals out · real city
Day 11 morning — Ferry approach to Amalfi
Camera out before you arrive. The coast from the water is the frame — cliffs, town stacked into hillside, colour everywhere. Moving deck, one hand possible. X100VI earns its place. Positano visible on the way. Travelmar or Alicost, ~35-40 min from Salerno port (Molo Manfredi).
🚢 Morning ferry · coastal light · X100VI
Day 11 — Amalfi Cathedral staircase
The family climbing toward you from below, or you above them looking down as they approach. The town drops away behind them. Hard midday light creates strong shadow on the stairs — work with it. The facade is striking enough to earn a straight architectural frame too.
☀ Midday · hard light · use the shadow geometry
Day 11 afternoon — Amalfi beach + long lunch
Jayden and Kai in the water. This is the relaxed family documentation shot — nobody performing for the camera. Shoot from the beach edge, let them play. Long lunch on the waterfront — table, food, the coast behind. Ektacolor made for this light.
🌊 Beach light · afternoon · family at ease

Naples
Day 12
Jun 17 · 1 night · Last day
Day 12 afternoon — Spaccanapoli
The long straight street that cuts through the old city. Louder, grittier, more alive than anywhere else on this trip. Street food everywhere — fried pizza, sfogliatella. Let the kids eat everything. Don't try to control it. React. This is where your Tokyo instinct serves you most. Last major film opportunity of the trip — shoot freely.
🌆 Afternoon · street life peaks · reactive mode
Day 12 evening — Chiaia waterfront · Vesuvius across the bay
Via Caracciolo at last light. The volcano silhouetted across the water. Family at the railing. This is a once-in-a-trip frame — last night in Italy, last meaningful film moment. Don't miss it. Then Neapolitan pizza dinner — the table, the food, the faces. Shoot through dinner.
🌅 Last light of the trip · Vesuvius · make it count

Film Mindset · Italy
You have 15 rolls. That's 540 frames. Shoot freely — you will not run out.
After the MP, the iPhone comes up. One photo. Location · shutter · aperture · one note. 20 seconds. That's the log.
Ektacolor handles highlights gently. In hard Italian midday sun — expose for the shadows, let the highlights roll off. The film will hold it.
The 28mm is your eye. It's always on. Only reach for 35mm or 50mm when you feel it — don't think about it.
You're not slowing the family down. You're documenting them. The camera is already around your neck. The shot takes 2 seconds. Trust that.
